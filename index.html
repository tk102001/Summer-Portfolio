TANVI KARWA — MSAUD 2026 Prologue

A Summer at GSAPP · 2026

This summer changed how I see cities.

A record of what shifted — in the way I look, draw, argue, and imagine — across ten weeks of urban design.

Scroll
01

Arrival

I arrived in New York believing urban design was mostly about drawing better buildings — that if a place felt wrong, the fix was formal. I had a sketchbook full of plans and almost no questions.

The first site visit undid that. Standing at street level, I realized I had been designing cities from the plan view my whole life — never from the sidewalk.

02

Learning to Observe

Observation, I learned, is not passive. A mental map is already an argument about what matters. A field note is already a design decision.

What I noticed changed. I stopped looking for what was missing and started looking for what was already working, quietly, without being designed.

03

Learning to Represent

Representation stopped being the last step and became the thinking itself. A section drawn wrong taught me the site was wrong in my head.

04

Studio

The studio project is what everyone sees. The eleven versions before it are what actually taught me. This is one of them.

Between v1 and the final, the project stopped being about the object I was placing on the site, and started being about the relationships I was setting up around it.

06

Who am I becoming?

A designer who starts from the ground, not the plan. Who asks who gets to decide before asking what gets built.

07

Questions I carry forward

  • Whose city am I actually designing for, and how would I know if I got it wrong?
  • Where is the line between intervention and imposition?
  • Can representation ever be neutral — or is choosing what to draw already a form of power?
  • What does urban design owe to a place after the render is finished?
